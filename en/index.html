Your knowledge base, kept by Claude

A folder of text files where Claude keeps what you tell it —suppliers, contracts, accounts, decisions, open items—, each fact with its date and its source, and which warns you about what is about to expire and what does not add up.

You tell it something→It files it in its place→It updates what is related→It warns you if it contradicts something

Some examples of what you can keep

Everyone builds their own base, with their own topics. These eleven are examples we have already worked through: when you paste the initial prompt, Claude offers them, asks whether any of them fits you and what other topics you want to keep. Keep several, none, or start with your own.

Suppliers and contacts

Who is who and what each one does.

"Who handles our support for X?"

Contracts and expiry dates

Dates, notice periods and renewals.

"What expires in six months?"

Invoices

Each one linked to its contract.

"What are we paying with no contract?"

Budget

Lines, planned and actual, checked against contracts.

"Which lines go up, and why?"

Receipts and expenses

Every card charge with its receipt.

"Which charges have no receipt?"

Access to services

Address and account. Never passwords.

"Which account do we log in with?"

Domains, certificates and subscriptions

The renewal calendar.

"What renews this quarter?"

Procedures

How each thing is done, step by step.

"How do we set up a new user?"

Projects and decisions

Status, milestones and the why.

"Why did we rule that out?"

Inventories

Equipment and licenses, always dated.

"How many licenses do we have?"

Articles and communication

How you write, what you have published and the drafts.

"Write me a draft in my style"

What pays off most is cross-checking them: an invoice with no contract, a contract with no budget line or a supplier who bills you and is not in the directory only show up when everything is in the same place. What each example sets up.

Three principles

1

Capture it when it happens

You tell Claude in the chat, with no forms. What is not written down at the time gets lost.

2

Every fact, with a date and proof

Without a date you cannot tell if it is out of date; without a source you cannot check it.

3

One source, with an owner

Each thing lives in one document, and the others link to it instead of copying it.

An open format, not an app

The documents follow the Open Knowledge Format (OKF) v0.2, an open specification from Google Cloud designed so that the same knowledge can be understood by people and by AI agents. It is deliberately minimal: Markdown files with a header, no central registry and no required tools.

Readable without tools

Text files any editor can open, today and in ten years.

An agent understands it as is

Claude reads and writes it directly, with no adapters and no database.

Every change shows

A change is a text diff, in git or in your cloud's version history.

It is yours and moves with you

It depends on no app: switch tools and take it all with you.

Every fact, with its source, its date and how reliable it is

A base kept by an agent has to say not only what it knows, but where it knows it from and how much it is worth. OKF solves it with fields in the header; this method, with rules Claude applies to every fact.

The questionIn OKF v0.2In this method
Where does it come from?sourcesEvery fact carries its proof: the original is linked and, if you enable the archive, kept
How far can I trust it?verified"Signed" only with proof; what is missing is "to confirm", never made up
Is it still true?stale_afterEvery perishable fact is dated, and the health check looks for old ones
Is it current?statusOne source per topic, and a log of what changed and when
Does anything contradict it?—If two facts clash, both are noted with their source

What we learned by breaking it

One false alarm switches off the rest

Before marking something urgent, check whether it renews by itself.

What is answered in the chat evaporates

If an answer took work, file it as a document.

The base only knows what goes through it

Five minutes a week telling it what did not come up in the chat.

Suggest improvements

This comes from a real base and gets better with what each person brings: a rule that is not needed, an example that is missing, a simpler way to explain something or a technology that helps. Leave it in the repository's issues (a free GitHub account is needed).
